## 1. The publisher with a territorial policy: Copernicus Publications

Verified verbatim from https://publications.copernicus.org/for_authors/manuscript_preparation.html
(checked 2026-08-05):

> "Please adhere to United Nations naming conventions for maps used in your
> manuscript." … "In order to depoliticize scientific articles, authors should
> avoid the drawing of borders or use of contested topographical names." … "The
> editors reserve the right to insert the label 'under dispute' if contested
> borders are presented."

**Honesty caveats:**
- This is a **maps/borders check, not an affiliation check** — Copernicus polices
  cartography in figures, not "Simferopol, Russian Federation" in author metadata.
  No major publisher we found (Springer Nature, Elsevier, Wiley, IEEE, MDPI, IOP)
  has an affiliation-level sovereignty check.
- Not confirmed to be the publisher previously referenced in our materials — no
  mention of Copernicus/Pensoft found in repo docs, the PLOS ONE manuscript, or
  the KI article (which states the opposite: "Reputable publishers such as Wiley,
  Elsevier, and IOP also effectively legalize annexation"). **Confirm with Ivan
  whether Copernicus is the one he remembers.**
- Under UN naming conventions, Crimea is Ukraine (UNGA 68/262) — so Copernicus's
  policy, uniquely, imports the de jure standard into peer review.

## 2. The stolen-facilities research to bring in

**Primary source (obtained and read, 26 pp.):**
Plastun, A., & Kozmenko, S. (2025). *Stolen Ukrainian universities: An invisible
russian weapon.* Problems and Perspectives in Management, 23(2-si), 151–175.
doi:10.21511/ppm.23(2-si).2025.11 (open access, CC-BY; published 3 Feb 2025;
special issue "Ukrainian Universities in New Realities: 10 Years of War").

Key verified numbers (from the paper itself):
- **1,536** educational institutions/authorities located or relocated 2014–2024
  from Luhansk, Donetsk, Zaporizhzhia, Kherson, Kharkiv Oblasts and Crimea
  (source: USEDE state database via Inforesurs).
- **289 stolen institutions of higher education**; by region: Donetsk 105,
  **Crimea 88**, Luhansk 64, Kherson 18, Zaporizhzhia 14. Status: 242 "blocked",
  53 "moved".
- Crimea 2013: ~100 scientific institutions incl. **20 branches of the National
  Academy of Sciences of Ukraine — all stolen** (Kubijovyč 2022 via Plastun).
  16 universities, up to 63,000 students (Roache 2018 via Plastun).
- Dose-response by occupation duration (Plastun et al. 2024a, Scopus): publications
  marking the territory as Russian exceed **90% for Crimean cities** (occupied
  2014) vs ~50% for Donetsk/Luhansk (occupied 2014, "annexed" 2022) vs Mariupol
  0% (2022) → 4% (2023). **Kherson: two Scopus-indexed articles affiliated Kherson
  with Russia in 2024 — two years after liberation.**
- International publishers carried **over 30% of problematic-affiliation
  publications in 2023**: Springer Nature/Pleiades (DE/US), MDPI (CH),
  EDP Sciences (FR), IEEE (US), Elsevier.

### The stolen facilities presented as Russian in Scopus (named in the paper)

Ukrainian institutions seized in Crimea that **Scopus now indexes as Russian**:
- Crimean Astrophysical Observatory → "Crimean Astrophysical Observatory RAS"
- Marine Hydrophysical Institute (NASU) → "Marine Hydrophysical Institute of RAS"
- A.O. Kovalevsky Institute of Biology of the Southern Seas (NASU) →
  "…of Russian Academy of Science"
- Nikitsky Botanical Gardens → "National Scientific Center of Russian Academy of
  Sciences"
- "Magarach" Institute of Grape Growing and Wine-making → "All-Russian National
  Research Institute of Viticulture and Winemaking 'Magarach' RAS"
- "Black Sea Hydrophysical Proving Ground RAS", "Institute of Crimean
  Archaeology" (Scopus, 2024)

This is the physical-world counterpart of our metadata findings: the buildings,
instruments, herbaria, and journals are Ukrainian state property; their scientific
output now enters CrossRef/Scopus/C4/LLM training data under Russian affiliations.

Paper's recommendations (align with ours): remove stolen universities from
affiliations and databases; revoke ISSN registrations of stolen Ukrainian
journals; COPE should issue ethical guidelines preventing collaboration with
stolen universities.

**To verify before citing (not yet confirmed from primary source):** the widely
quoted "Scopus locates Sevastopol in Russia in 99% / Simferopol 93% of
publications" figures — attributed to the companion study Plastun et al. (2024a),
"How Russia uses science to justify the annexation of Ukrainian territories"
(Knowledge and Performance Management, Business Perspectives). Our PDF states
">90% for Crimean cities"; pull the 2024a PDF for the exact per-city numbers.

## 3. The metadata-war win (positive counterexample for the policy article)

Publishing Perspectives (Apr 2025), "A Win in Ukraine's Academic 'Metadata War'":
- **ISSN International Centre** implemented technical measures making the actual
  source of hijacked/stolen journals visible; achieved via cooperation of the
  Ukrainian National ISSN Centre, EASE Ukraine, SUPRR (Frances Pinter), and
  Ukraine's Ministry of Education and Science (Denys Kurbatov quoted).
- The **Ukrainian national ISSN Centre refuses ISSN services** to entities in
  occupied territories.

Together with the plotly/ECharts/amCharts overrides (`docs/ne_propagation_graph.md`)
and Copernicus's UN-conventions rule, this completes the argument: **every layer
of the pipeline — geodata, libraries, registries, publishers — has at least one
actor proving the fix is feasible. The rest choose not to.**
